# FacadeOps research guides

## Property-manager interview (30 minutes)

Do not pitch during the first 20 minutes. Ask for recent examples and artifacts, not opinions about imagined features.

1. Tell me about the buildings and exterior-maintenance decisions you are responsible for.
2. Think of the last time you needed to understand an exterior condition. What triggered it?
3. What did you do first, and who became involved?
4. What access method was used? Why was it chosen?
5. What information did you receive at the end? Ask to see a redacted example if appropriate.
6. What was still uncertain after the inspection or maintenance work?
7. Where do photographs, reports, quotations, and completion evidence live today?
8. How do you compare a condition with the previous inspection?
9. What caused delay, extra cost, disruption, or internal disagreement?
10. Who approves an inspection and who approves subsequent work?
11. What would make a structured recurring exterior record useful enough to pay for?
12. Would you be willing to review a synthetic sample report or request a scoped site assessment?

Record building type and role, but do not collect resident names, apartment numbers, access codes, precise security arrangements, or unredacted property records.

## Operator / regulatory / insurance specialist interview

1. Which authorizations and records are required for a commercial exterior inspection in Luanda?
2. Which requirements change by aircraft class, VLOS/BVLOS operation, location, height, or proximity to controlled/restricted airspace?
3. What must a customer provide before feasibility can be assessed?
4. What aircraft, pilot, maintenance, and flight records should a coordinator verify?
5. Which insurance policies and limits are customary or mandatory?
6. What site-control, public-protection, and emergency arrangements are required?
7. Which weather and visibility limits commonly cancel a mission?
8. What privacy and imagery restrictions should be contractual?
9. What scope can be priced without a site assessment?
10. What evidence may be delivered to a technical reviewer, and in what format?
11. What claims should FacadeOps never make on behalf of an operator?
12. Would you review the draft go/no-go checklist and a synthetic job scenario?

## Technical reviewer / facade contractor interview

1. Which exterior conditions can reasonably be observed from imagery?
2. Which conclusions require touch, measurement, sampling, access equipment, or structural analysis?
3. What image angle, distance, scale, resolution, and location metadata are useful?
4. How should uncertainty and inaccessible areas be represented?
5. Which classification system would support maintenance decisions without creating false precision?
6. What separates a useful condition record from a folder of photographs?
7. How should findings map to additional investigation, monitoring, cleaning, or repair?
8. Which record would help a contractor quote accurately?
9. Which professional credentials or sign-offs are required for specific conclusions?
10. Would you review a pilot report and accept responsibility for the technical interpretation you provide?

## Evidence capture

After each interview, record: participant category, date, consent status, problem example, current method, decision owner, artifacts used, frequency, cost/disruption evidence if voluntarily supplied, strongest quotation cleared for internal use, follow-up request, and contradictions. Store contact details separately from notes intended for analysis.
